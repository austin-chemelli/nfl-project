# ADR 0019: League page: team charts, standings, simulated playoff odds

Status: accepted 2026-11-05 (Austin). Closes hub decision D9. Research: `.notes/plans/league-page.md` and `.notes/plans/team-scatter.md` (uncommitted; every number below was measured there on the data of 2026-11-05). Tasks: M14.

## Context
The site answers "who wins this game" and "is the model any good", but nothing answers "where does the league stand": no standings, no playoff odds, no way to see all 32 teams at once. Austin asked for playoff predictions with game leverage, historical look-alike teams, and scatter charts of any two features. The data supports all of it today: `curated/games` has the full 2026 schedule, the feature store has a row for every remaining game (the Student scores all 151 in 0.2 s), and 24 seasons of results since the 2002 realignment sit on disk. Two things are missing from the pipeline: a division and conference map (nothing in the repo carries one; only `div_game`) and any standings code.

Constraints: open data only (ADR 0001), additive contracts (ADR 0015), the market stays the reference (ADR 0013), no logos (ADR 0011), one answer first and detail behind URL tabs (ADR 0017), and the site must stay honest that playoff odds inherit the Student's gap to the market.

## Options
1. **Tiebreakers.** (a) Approximate: head-to-head, then division or conference record, then a coin. (b) The real procedure through strength of schedule, then a seeded coin flip. A 200-line scratch engine for (b) reproduced the actual seeds in 48 of 48 conference-seasons from 2002 to 2025; no real tie went past strength of victory; (a) would have coin-flipped 18 of the 64 ties that decided a title or a playoff spot. (b) costs little more and is M, not L.
2. **Game probabilities in the simulation.** (a) Fixed per-game probabilities, treating a team's games as independent. (b) A per-run team-strength shock on each team's logit. In a frozen-Elo backtest (2005–2025, 672 team-seasons) the shock cut log loss on P(playoffs) from 0.420 to 0.405 at σ ≈ 0.7; without it the >90% bucket predicted 0.966 and saw 0.885.
3. **The market view.** (a) Only where a vig-free line exists (29 of 151 games today). (b) Fill the rest with ratings fitted to market prices, labeled with counts. (c) No market view. ADR 0013 makes the market the reference, so (c) hides the honest comparison and (a) is a page that mostly says "no line yet".
4. **Where team charts live.** (a) Their own `/teams` page. (b) A tab on `/league`. The two share the division map, the team panel, `?team=` and the freshness line, and the nav is already cut off on phones at six items.
5. **Odds over time.** (a) Append-only snapshots from launch. (b) Backfill Weeks 1–8 with Elo. The store has future-game rows only from 2026-10-27, so a Student snapshot for Week 5 cannot be rebuilt honestly, and (b) mixes two models in one chart.

## Decision
- **One page, `/league`, nav label "League" after "This week"**, tabs Teams · Playoff odds · Standings · Games that matter · Similar seasons. It ships in three phases (M14): the Teams tab first, the playoff picture second, context third. Until phase 2 lands, the page shows only the Teams tab and no summary strip, like `/hypotheses`.
- **Standings and tiebreakers are the real procedure** (`nfl/league/tiebreak.py`): steps 1–6 (head-to-head or sweep, division record, common games with the four-game minimum, conference record, strength of victory, strength of schedule) with restarts after an elimination, for division and wild-card ties, then a seeded coin flip. "Seed today" on real results also runs the points-based steps 7–10, since scores exist; simulated games have no scores, which is the natural cap. The fine print says so and quotes the 48 of 48 check.
- **A static division and conference map** lives in `nfl/league/divisions.py`, with `TEAM_ALIASES` for STL, SD and OAK, and a test that the 2026 `div_game` pairs form exactly its eight groups. The web app reads conference and division from the JSON, never from its own map.
- **Playoff odds by Monte Carlo**: 10,000 runs per variant, each run drawing every remaining game, with a per-team strength shock δ ~ N(0, σ²) added to the logit for all of that team's games, σ calibrated on a backtest (M14.9) and stored as a constant with its evidence. Simulated ties are ignored. The playoffs are simulated through the Super Bowl with ratings fitted to each variant's own probabilities (home term kept, zero for the Super Bowl). Outputs per team: P(playoffs), P(division), P(bye), P(seed k), P(each round), P(champion), mean wins and the p10–p90 wins range. Never 0% or 100%: "<0.1%" and ">99.9%". Monte Carlo never says "clinched" or "eliminated"; exact clinch logic is a later item.
- **Two variants, side by side: the Student and the market.** The Student uses its locked row for a game where one exists, else `score_approaches` as of the build. The market uses the vig-free moneyline where a line exists, else ratings fitted to market prices (upcoming lines plus this season's closing moneylines), with the counts of each source published and shown.
- **Game leverage** comes from the same runs: for each game and team, P(playoffs | home wins) and P(playoffs | away wins), reported only when both branches hold at least 1,000 runs and the swing is at least 5 points. Games rank by the summed absolute swing. The slate card gets one line from the Student's variant only, for the team with the largest swing, when it is at least 8 points.
- **Similar seasons are descriptive.** Nearest neighbours by games played (not calendar week) over seven standardized season-to-date features with the record weighted double, candidates 2002–2025. Show the 10 nearest by name; summarize over the 25 nearest (leave-one-season-out Brier 0.131 for "made the playoffs" against a 0.238 base). The page says "teams that looked like this after N games finished like this", never "this team will".
- **Team charts**: `site/teams.json` holds every team's latest value, league percentile and rank for every team-level feature, built from each team's **next-game** row as of the last feature build, so numbers change when the features do and not when a game kicks off (the `feature_bands` bug: it took the last-kickoff row, a Week 18 pregame row, so six rate features and every per-game feature were stale on the game page; `feature_bands` now reads the same table). The registry gains `Member.short` (axis labels, ≤ 22 characters) and publishes a `better` direction, null for context-only groups and for luck and strength of schedule. The chart is hand-rolled SVG on `d3-scale` with the `useChartTip` card, eight preset pairs with quadrant captions, deterministic label placement that hides a label rather than overlap it, team colors by default (amended below), a tap-to-highlight division and no logos. Clicking a team opens an in-page panel with its games; there is no team route yet.
- **Artifacts and jobs.** `site/teams.json` and `site/league.json` are written inside `site.build` (try/except, like retros), so every refresh, lock, odds run and `nfl-site` run refreshes them; about 25 s per build. Snapshots append to `marts/league/season=YYYY/snapshots.parquet` only on the Tuesday refresh and after each real lock, never edited, no backfill; the odds-history series starts the week phase 2 ships. The RNG seed derives from the season, the last result and the input hash, so a rebuild on unchanged inputs is identical.

## Amendment 2026-11-05: team colors on the scatter
Austin, after the first look at the Teams tab: why blue and orange instead of team colors? The original reason held for one color per team: on the dark theme 107 of the 496 team pairs land under 8 on the OKLab scale and three pairs are identical (DAL and NE, ATL and TB, CIN and DEN). A second brand color fixes most of that, so a dot is now the team's main color inside a ring of its second color (`teamDot` in `lib/teamColors.ts`, both fitted per theme). Pairs that stay under 8 on both colors fall to 21 on dark and 14 on light; those teams really do share colors (DAL and IND), and the three-letter label names them. Team is the default, Conference stays a choice with its legend, and a division highlight keeps its four teams' colors against gray. The label stays the sure way to name a team, in every mode and for every reader.

## Amendment 2026-11-05: what phase 2 measured
- **Shock:** `SHOCK_SIGMA = 0.4`, from `scripts/league_backtest.py` over 2010 to 2025 at 6, 9 and 12 games played. For the Student proxy the log loss on P(playoffs) falls from 0.3589 at σ = 0 to 0.3541, a gain of 0.0047 with a 95% season-bootstrap interval of −0.0123 to +0.0021, so it can't be told from noise at any cut. Frozen Elo gains clearly (σ = 0.5: −0.0120, interval −0.0215 to −0.0029; best at 0.7). Every σ shares one random stream, so the comparison is paired. The Student proxy is already calibrated at the top (predicts 97%, sees 96% at σ = 0). Historical Student rows know every result before their own week, so the backtest fits ratings to rows before the cut and prices the rest from those: it is a proxy, not the live Student's forward rows.
- **The market fill-in is weak.** Lines reach about a week ahead, so 122 of 151 games use ratings fitted to this season's lines. Fitted without the 29 real lines, those ratings miss them by 6.9 points of win chance a game (worst 28.5), and the Student misses them by 5.8. The half-life makes no difference (14 days to none: 6.9 to 7.0). So the planned tile "Student and market differ most" is dropped: its 35-point headline (TB) was mostly the fit. The Market view says how few games have a line, and the fine print quotes this test.
- **Playoff round odds** price from ratings fitted on played and remaining games: on remaining games alone they shrink to nothing by Week 18. After the last regular-season game `league.json` keeps the final standings with no odds, because the engine can't start from playoff results.
- **Tabs:** Austin, on seeing it: Playoff odds is the default and first tab, Teams second, because the strip above it is about the playoffs. Links from before this need `?tab=teams`.
- **Contract:** `odds.<variant>` is `{sources, teams}` rather than team keys beside a `_sources` key, and `games[]` adds `home`, `away`, `kickoff` and the branch run counts.

## Amendment 2026-11-06: what phase 3 measured
Similar seasons score a leave-one-season-out Brier of 0.134 at k = 25 and 8 games played for "made the playoffs" (n = 768; base 0.238), matching the plan's 0.134. A team under four games played has no entry. The current vector comes from `team_latest`, the same next-game rows as `teams.json`, so it moves with a feature build and not with a kickoff. It lives in its own `site/similar.json` (187 KB, 20 KB gzipped, about 0.5 s a build), so the slate doesn't load it, and a failure costs the tab, not the odds.

Austin, on the first version: it showed who was similar but not why, with no real visual, and he wanted red flags and things that look bad but aren't. So the tab gains an overlap chart and flag cards.

Flags come from a fixed list, never searched for at build time. The study (`scripts/league_flags.py`): among teams on exactly the same record, 2002 to 2025 at 6, 8, 10 and 12 games, a measure's worst or best quarter of its season against the rest, season bootstrap, Benjamini-Hochberg at 10%, then a 2002-13 against 2014-25 check. The list was chosen after seeing those results and the halves reuse the same seasons, so nothing is a held-out test, and the fine print says so. Kept: red for offense (−6.4 points of playoff chance), expected win rate (−4.6) and QB play (−4.9, mostly the newer half); strengths for long-run rating (+16.1 points of reaching a conference game), QB play and offense; "looks bad, wasn't" for a record that undersells the team (+7.1, a real effect) and three nulls, no penalty found: last three games, streak and turnover margin. Rejected: defense (raw −1.3, but opponent-adjusted, which the chart uses, −3.4 [−7.2, +0.2], too close to a penalty to call fine), point margin (its conference-game effect isn't significant), EPA trend (flips sign between halves), rushing offense, special teams, high luck.

Rules for a card: a quarter is within a season, because EPA levels drift, by the same average rank for history and for this team. Peers are exactly the same record; a range of records put weak teams at its low end, and CLE's −35 points was really −13. Cards show only from 6 to 12 games: from 13 the effects fade or flip. A card shows only if the team's own counts agree with its kind by at least 2 points, and neither the record alone settles it (both rates under 10% or over 90%). On real data that is 32 cards, with 20 of 32 teams showing at least one. A card says what separated teams on a record, never that a measure matters or doesn't on its own.

## Consequences
- New modules `nfl/league/` (divisions, teams file, standings, tiebreak, simulate, inputs, similar, build) and `nfl league`; two new site files; one new mart. Architecture §3.6d and §3.7.
- The game page's Details bands change slightly for FTN rates and per-game features once `feature_bands` reads next-game rows. Intended.
- Playoff odds inherit everything the Student lacks: form is frozen, injuries are league-average beyond this week, and its gap to the market. Showing the market variant beside it is the honesty guard, and the fine print says what each variant used.
- Every site build gets about 25 s slower. If the six weekly `odds` runs mind, a flag can skip the league build there.
- The 2026 playoff format and tiebreak text are taken from NFL.com as of 2026-11-05; a rule change would need the engine updated and the replay test re-run.
- Later, not now: exact clinch logic; a `teams_history_<season>.json` with a week slider (the team object's shape is kept so it is a loop, not a redesign); per-team routes; the tendencies as scatter axes (a second source).
