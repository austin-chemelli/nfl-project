# ADR 0028: Matchups and paths to an upset: situation splits as context, conditions not probabilities, edges only through a separate approach

Status: accepted 2026-11-09 (Austin; hub decisions D26–D29). Extends ADR 0018 (context-only groups, the bar for a public line), ADR 0024 (template sentences, strong calls), ADR 0025 (SQL models), ADR 0026 (the candidates ledger as the one evidence gate). Closes M1.13 and takes over M23 phase 2. Brief and measurements: `.notes/matchup-insights-direction.md`, `.notes/plans/interaction-features.md`. Tasks: M25.

## Context
Austin's direction (2026-11-09): the site previews games well and the spread sitting near the market is fine. The interesting product is **insight**: how each offense does in the situations this opponent creates (against the blitz, a stacked box, play action; by personnel and coverage), where the matchups line up and where they don't, a credible account of how the underdog wins even when we don't pick it, and disagreements with the market with their reasons. Splits start as context and reach the predictions only on evidence. Example: GB at NE, Week 9 (NE −3): GB won big; Austin's read beforehand was that GB's defense was good and NE had only beaten weak teams.

What the data says (all measured on our plays, 2006–2025 unless noted):
- **Tendencies repeat, results in a situation don't.** Split-half r: blitz rate 0.75, motion 0.91, play action 0.64; the EPA gap a team shows against the blitz −0.08, against 5+ rushers −0.04, with play action 0.02, against a light box 0.14 (2026-11-05). Pressure is the exception (year-to-year 0.43), and it comes from participation, which is published after the season.
- **"Beats bad teams, struggles against good ones" isn't a trait.** An offense's gap between strong and weak defenses: split-half −0.06, year-to-year 0.01 over 550 team-seasons; plain opponent-adjusted offense repeats at 0.63. Through Week 8 NE's offense was 9th raw and adjusted against a league-average schedule; GB's defense 7th. Nothing we hold would have called the blowout. What the site could have shown is which matchup decided the game and how GB wins.
- **Upset conditions separate winners sharply after the fact and barely at all before.** 4,650 games with a spread. A dog of 3–6.5 wins 37%; one that wins the turnover battle by one wins 69% (it happens in 29% of those games), one with the early-down EPA edge 67%, one with more explosive plays 56%, one that sacks the favorite two more times 63%. Dogs that won held 2.5 of five conditions on average; dogs that lost 0.9. Before the game, both teams' season-to-date rates predict the game's turnover margin with r 0.00 (slope 0.01), explosives r 0.16, early-down EPA r 0.17, sacks r 0.15. Added to the spread, the four together change the log loss of "dog wins" by −0.0001. The market has priced what is predictable; what decides upsets is mostly not.
- **In season, coverage, personnel, pressure and time to throw are last season's numbers** (participation 2016–2025, published each February). FTN charting (blitz, rushers, box, motion, play action, screens, RPO, pocket) and play-by-play situations (shotgun, down, field zone, game script, pass depth, opponent tier) are live, one to two days after games.
- Two things already work and are the right hooks: the strong-calls ledger (ADR 0024) grades our disagreements with the market; the candidates ledger (ADR 0026) says which stats the market under-weights (offensive success rate +0.58 points per SD against the market's residual, defense at half weight +0.34).

## Options
1. **Framing of "paths to an upset".** (a) A probability: an upset model from pre-game paths. (b) Conditions and plausibility: what winning dogs of this size did, how often, and how often each team produces or allows it, with the honest reliability of each. (c) Leave it to the retro.
2. **Where the split numbers live.** (a) The feature store (`build_all`, runs unguarded inside the lock). (b) A SQL model under `marts/derived/` plus a pure module, read by the site, the team files and the retro; no lock code.
3. **What a team's split shows.** (a) The raw number. (b) The team's gap shrunk toward the league-wide effect of the situation (empirical Bayes, k fitted on earlier seasons only), with raw, n and a reliability badge beside it.
4. **Coverage and personnel.** (a) Last season's numbers, labeled "2025". (b) Out until the 2026 participation data lands.
5. **Disagreements with the market.** (a) The strong calls as they are. (b) Plus a per-game "lean" from the ledger: the `use`-verdict pregame candidates' slopes against the market times this game's gaps, in points with the ledger's CI.
6. **How edges reach a line.** (a) Members of the Student at a refit. (b) A separate `matchup` approach beside the Student (the `student_v2` pattern), after the ledger and a shadow record. (c) Never.

## Decision
1(b), 2(b), 3(b), 4(b), 5(b), 6(b).

- **Situation splits are a SQL model, `team_split_week`** (one row per season, week, game, team, side, split, flag: plays, EPA, success, explosive plays), with the split definitions registered once in `src/nfl/splits.py` (label, who chooses the situation, source, first season) and a test that the SQL and the registry agree. v1 splits use columns the plays table already has: blitz, 5+ rushers, light box (≤6) and heavy box (≥7), play action, motion, screen, RPO, shotgun vs under center, no-huddle, early downs vs third down, field zone, neutral game script (win probability 20–80%), deep passes (air yards ≥15), opponent tier (top-ten defense or offense by adjusted EPA as of the week). No curate change, no lock code.
- **The matchup engine** (`src/nfl/matchups.py`, pure functions) reads the model as of the week (`week < w`, seasons before for the prior). Per unit and split: raw EPA with and without, n, the league gap as of the week, the shrunk gap (`k_f = σ²/τ²` by method of moments on earlier seasons, clamped to [400, 5000]; 2000 where no earlier FTN season exists), the opponent's rate and the rate already faced, and the edge in points per game: `(opponent rate − rate faced) × shrunk gap × base plays`. When the team's residual shrinks to nothing the edge is the opponent's habit times the league effect, which is the honest result and still an insight ("NE blitzes 38%; the league loses 0.04 EPA a dropback against the blitz; GB has seen 27%").
- **Paths to an upset are conditions, never a probability (D29).** `src/nfl/paths.py` and a SQL model `upset_conditions` (dog size bucket × condition → dogs' win rate and n, from every game with a spread since 2006) give each game: the underdog by the market line; five conditions (turnover margin +1, early-down EPA edge, more explosive plays, two more sacks of the favorite, special teams +3); for each, the historical win rate at this size, how often this dog has produced it and this favorite has allowed it this season, and a plausibility label fixed by the pre-game measurement above (`coin flip for every team` for turnovers, `weakly predictable` for the rest). One template sentence, "How GB wins: …", every number from the data (ADR 0024's rule). The headline chance does not move. After the game, the same conditions are checked against the box and the sentence says which happened.
- **Market leans from the ledger (D29).** For each game, the ledger's latest row per candidate with verdict `use` and `pregame` true gives a slope against the market in points per SD; the lean is the sum over those candidates of slope × this game's standardized home-minus-away gap, with the CI carried through. Shown beside the strong calls as "what the market tends to miss here", live from 2026 Week 10 with weeks tracked, graded on the fly for finished games (which side of the market line the margin landed on). It is context: no line, no ledger row, no README.
- **Every second-level number carries the `ReliabilityBadge`** (M23.8b) from a ledger row: each split's shrunk gap is a candidate (`split_<name>`), screened for split-half r, and the badge reads stable / some / noise from it. A split with no ledger row shows `noise`.
- **Coverage, personnel, pressure and time to throw are out of M25 (D27).** They return as an offseason view when the 2026 participation file lands; the pressure prior from `.notes/plans/interaction-features.md` waits with them.
- **Edges reach a line only through a separate approach (D28).** Phase 3, offseason, gated: the edges are game-level candidates in the ledger (kind `spread`, the net edge home minus away) plus the matchup-specific gate (the edge predicts the team-game EPA residual, walk-forward, slope ≥ 0.3 with a CI excluding 0). If they pass, a `matchup` feature family and a `matchup.net_edge_pg` member make `matchups` a weighted group at registry v7, listed by one config, `configs/matchup.yaml`, and by no other; the Student never carries it. The approach is fitted and backfilled with `--only`, deployed by the runbook's "Adding an approach mid-season", and shown beside the Student like `student_v2`. This amends ADR 0018 for that one group and that one approach.
- **Pages.** The game's Tendencies tab becomes "Matchups" (tab id unchanged for URLs): a `MatchupGrid` per side (rows are situations; the opponent's habit on the league band, this unit's shrunk result with n, the edge colored by who it favors, the badge), then the existing tendency rows. The Preview gains an `UpsetPaths` card under "Who has the edge". "Where we differ from the market" gains the lean. The team page's Tendencies tab gains "By situation" (`Dumbbell` rows, offense and defense). The retro gains the in-game splits against season to date and the paths' outcomes.

## Consequences
- Phases 0–2 are site and derived-data work: no change under `features/`, `approaches/` or `lock.py`, so they can merge in season at any hour. Phase 3 is lock code and waits for the offseason.
- The honest numbers are small: typical edges are a few tenths of a point a game, most badges will read `noise` on team splits, and the turnover condition will say "coin flip" for every team. The reliability badge and the plausibility label are part of the deliverable, not polish; without them the page would present noise as insight.
- Nothing here changes a public line or the headline chance. The lean and the paths are explanations with a record, like the strong calls and the total.
- The retro job starts ingesting `ftn_charting` so the morning-after splits are charted; a failure there costs the splits, never a lock.
- The 7 planned `matchup.*` members (groups.py, "planned" since v3) are superseded by the single `net_edge_pg`, if anything; `docs/feature-groups.md` says so.
- Open: the opponent-tier split uses our adjusted EPA as of the week, which makes "top-ten defense" a moving target early in a season; the grid hides it before Week 4.

## Measured
Filled in as M25 lands: the splits' split-half table (M25.2), the edge-residual gate (M25.8), the `matchup` approach's paired Δ (M25.11).

- **2026-11-09, planning (production copy, `scratchpad/m25/upset_paths.py`):** 4,650 regular-season games 2006–2025 with a spread and both box scores. Dog win rate by size: 0–2.5 46%, 3–6.5 37%, 7–9.5 23%, 10+ 14%. Conditional on the dog winning the turnover battle by one: 76%, 69%, 46%, 35% (it happens in 26–29% of games at each size); on the dog having the early-down EPA edge: 67% at 3–6.5; more explosive plays 56%; two more sacks of the favorite 63%; special teams +3 points 44%. Pre-game season-to-date rates (both teams, through the prior week, at least 3 games, n 3,793) against the game's realized margin: turnovers r +0.005, explosives +0.16, early-down EPA +0.17, sacks +0.15; all four added to the spread in a logistic for "dog wins" (dogs of 3+, n 2,981): Δ log loss −0.0001. Team-season spread of giveaways per 65 snaps: sd 0.41 around 1.46.
- **2026-11-10, M25.4 (`upset_conditions` SQL model, `paths.py`):** rebuilt from the plays, regular season 2006–2025, 5,195 dogs (the planning count of 4,650 dropped games for a reason not recorded). Dog win rate by size 46.1 / 36.9 / 22.8 / 13.8%. At 3–6.5: turnover margin ≥ +1 68.4%, early-down EPA edge 68.0% (EPA per play on downs 1–2 at win probability 0.2–0.8; without the filter 60%), more explosive plays 56.2%, two more sacks of the favorite 62.3%, special teams +3 43.1%; turnovers at the other sizes 76.0 / 46.3 / 34.9%. One correction: a turnover margin of +1 or better happens in 35% of 3–6.5 dogs' games (27–36% across sizes), not 26–29%; exactly +1 happens in 17% and wins 55%, so the planning number matched neither definition. The card uses ≥ +1.
- **2026-11-11, M25.2 (splits in the ledger):** one candidate per split, the unit that doesn't choose the situation (the offense for blitz and box, the defense for the offense-chosen splits), the with-minus-without EPA gap, at least 40 plays on each side per window, odd vs even weeks. Split-half r 2022–2025: blitz −0.09 (n 143, 90% CI −0.25 to +0.07; with a 20-play minimum −0.18, with 60 −0.04), light box 0.03 (not the 0.14 measured 2026-11-05), heavy box 0.03, play action −0.02, motion −0.02, shotgun 0.04, no-huddle 0.14 (the highest), early down 0.11, third down 0.08, red zone −0.06, own territory 0.00, neutral script 0.04, deep pass 0.02, opponent tier −0.01 / −0.03; screen and RPO too rare for 30 team-seasons at 40 plays (null). Every verdict `none`, every badge `noise`: team results in a situation don't repeat. Against the market's residual, motion's slope is +0.95 points per SD (90% CI 0.45 to 1.43) and deep pass −0.44 (−0.84 to −0.05), but neither is stable, so no lean uses them.
- **2026-11-12, redesign after Austin's review of #48 (amendment):** six more conditions, each "this team's offense beat the opponent's" on: EPA per dropback (pass game), 10-yard runs, third-down rate, rush success, 20-yard completions, red-zone TD rate. All dogs 2006–2025 (5,195, win 33%): pass game happens 37%, dogs who did it won 72%, pre-game r +0.20; big runs 52%, r +0.22; third down 52%, r +0.15; rush success 44%, r +0.13; deep passes 42%, r +0.14; red zone 40%, r +0.04 (labelled a coin flip). The last three are tier 2. The card no longer lists conditions: it shows up to four **levers**, conditions where the dog's unit ranks in the top half and at least eight places above the favorite's matching unit (season-to-date ranks from Week 4), tier 1 first and then by gap × lift, one per family (explosives, big runs and deep passes overlap); the other tier-1 conditions are chips (even, the favorite's edge, or neither strong). The Matchups tab leads with the same ranks as a face-off per side of the ball, keeps only the splits with an edge as worded habits, and folds the tendency charts in. Pass game and early downs partly describe the result itself (a team that passes well scores), so their win rates overstate what leaning on them buys.
